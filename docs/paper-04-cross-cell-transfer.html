PAPER 04 - Cross-Cell Transfer

Test in one cell type, predict the response in others.

Profile a compound once in an accessible source cell type, project its likely transcriptomic response in target cell types you never directly assayed, and use the projection to prioritize follow-up. A first practical tissue projection engine. Grouped chemical programs transfer at cosine 0.66 to 0.86, against an identity baseline of 0.42 to 0.52. The cost curve of running every program in every cell type begins to bend.

TL;DR

Direct reuse of a source-cell signature in a target cell type is inadequate (0.146 cosine in ZEL024 HEK293 to H1650). Learned ridge transfer doubles to triples that. The strongest current unit is the grouped chemical program: at chemistry-resolved aggregation, support-weighted cosine reaches 0.66 to 0.86 across four cell-pair directions, against an identity baseline of 0.42 to 0.52. That matches how early discovery decisions get made, at the level of chemical series, not isolated compounds.

Why it matters

A major share of preclinical cost goes to running the same compound, panel, or library in every cell type of practical interest. Cell-intrinsic pharmacology rarely transfers cleanly across tissues, so one reference cell line is usually not enough. Running ten cell lines for every campaign is what teams do when they have to.

This is the layer that begins to break that constraint. Profile a compound in one source cell type. Project its likely transcriptional response in target cell types you never measured. Use the projections to prioritize follow-up work, flag counter-screen liabilities, and decide where to spend wet-lab time. The claim is narrow on purpose: cross-cell transfer models cell-intrinsic transcriptional response. It does not substitute for pharmacokinetics, tissue architecture, or organism-level toxicology. It is upstream decision support, where even partial accuracy pays.

For a partner, the immediate read is operational: a small paired landmark atlas plus one source measurement begins to do useful work for every additional cell type of clinical interest. For an investor, the read is the cost curve. Instead of running every program through every cell type, the screen begins to project. The economics of preclinical efficacy start to bend in the same direction the platform's design has been pointing for years.

What we did

We measured the same combinatorial libraries across multiple cell lines in the canonical Z-Screen workspace, then asked how well a learned source-to-target transfer map could project a transcriptomic response into a target cell type held out at evaluation. The benchmark runs at two levels.

At the molecule level, we used the cell-pair systems with sufficient shared SMILES coverage: ZEL024 between HEK293 and H1650 (6,299 shared molecules) and ZEL031 between A549 and THP1 (3,561 shared molecules). Each molecule was represented by the mean of its 32-dimensional scVI latent coordinates within a given library and cell line. We compared direct reuse of the source-cell signature (identity baseline) against learned source-to-target ridge regression and PLS transfer maps.

At the grouped-program level, we restricted reporting to chemistry-resolved aggregations: BB-pair (bb0+bb1) signatures for the dense 4-position ZEL024 library, where each pair signature averages roughly 150 distinct tuples; and single-position bb0 signatures for the 2-position ZEL031 library, where each centroid still leaves the other position to vary across roughly 70 distinct partner chemistries. We did not report single-position averages for libraries with three or more substantive positions, because each centroid in those libraries averages over hundreds to thousands of distinct chemistries and any reported transfer cosine would be a measurement of library-wide rather than chemistry-specific behavior.

Centered-response cosine is the primary metric. Centering removes the constant baseline shift that exists between two cell lines regardless of any perturbation, so the number reflects perturbational response rather than bulk offset.

What we found

Five results that bend the cost curve of preclinical efficacy.

FINDING 01

Grouped chemical programs are the strongest unit of cross-cell transfer.

At chemistry-resolved aggregation, learned transfer reaches support-weighted cosine 0.863 for ZEL024 H1650 to HEK293 (84 shared BB-pairs, identity 0.471), 0.808 for the reverse direction, 0.714 for ZEL031 A549 to THP1 at single-position bb0 (129 shared groups, identity 0.419), and 0.664 for the reverse. Four directions, all in the 0.66 to 0.86 range, against identity baselines of 0.42 to 0.52.

0.863Best support-weighted cosine - ZEL024
FINDING 02

Direct reuse fails. Learned maps recover molecule-specific information.

Even at the molecule level, identity is inadequate: 0.146 centered cosine in ZEL024 (HEK293 to H1650) and 0.094 in ZEL031 (A549 to THP1). Ridge transfer roughly doubles to triples that: 0.279 (HEK293 to H1650), 0.371 (H1650 to HEK293), 0.242 (A549 to THP1). Cross-cell projection is a prediction problem, not an artifact of bulk offset.

2.5x identityRidge vs identity, ZEL024 H1650 to HEK293
FINDING 03

Performance saturates with paired landmark coverage.

Transfer accuracy rises quickly with shared training compounds and saturates within the available range. Useful accuracy shows up at around 5,000 paired molecules in the dense ZEL024 system, and continues to improve with scale. A small paired landmark atlas is enough to start projecting. The next investment widens coverage rather than tuning the model further.

~5K pairsWhere transfer becomes useful
FINDING 04

Cross-library projection defines the next dataset.

Training a transfer map on one library and testing on another in the same cell-pair direction was positive in some sparse settings (ZEL024 to ZEL028-2, HEK293 to H1650 lifted weighted cosine from 0.033 to 0.059) and weak or asymmetric in others. Sparse libraries are credible transfer targets in the current data, but unreliable transfer teachers. A small amount of additional collection in the sparse libraries would close this rung.

AsymmetricDense to sparse > sparse to dense
FINDING 05

Gene-level decoder turns latent transfer into ranked hypotheses.

An exploratory gene-level decoder, composed with the learned latent transfer, partially recovers held-out gene-rank structure. The strongest direction (ZEL024 H1650 to HEK293) reaches per-compound gene-rank Spearman 0.182 and top-50 absolute-delta overlap 0.139, against 0.021 and 0.083 when the source latent is passed directly through the target decoder. We treat this as a route to ranked gene lists from transferred latents. Encouraging, but not yet a primary endpoint.

Why this is different from the public references

L1000 and Tahoe-100M can compare. Only Z-Screen can compose.

LINCS L1000 and Tahoe-100M are valuable for comparing the response of a single compound across cell lines. Neither carries combinatorial-chemistry provenance. A hit is one profiled compound, with no internal structure to decompose. Z-Screen runs combinatorial libraries with full building-block provenance across multiple cell lines in the same canonical workspace. That is what makes chemistry-resolved grouped-program transfer possible at all. Without it, you can describe cross-cell behavior. You cannot compose it. With it, the cross-cell projection feeds directly into paper 05's CRISPR mapping in cell types where direct K562 or THP1 reference comparisons are impossible.
