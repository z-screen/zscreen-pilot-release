Z-Screen - Pilot Release - April 2026

A 'small' pilot, and what the data already reveals.

Z-Screen profiles combinatorial chemical libraries in 50,000-well chips by imaging and mRNA sequencing. The five preprints discussed here walk through learnings from a pilot dataset: the robustness of miniaturized multimodal readouts; the ability of large chemical libraries to probe, learn and predict cellular resonses; how far the predictions hold when the chemistry is changed; whether responses transfer across cell types; and whether drug mechanism-of-action can be inferred by comparing to CRISPR data. While a pilot, this data still represents the largest collection of small-molecule transcriptomics ever released, with imaging complementing mRNA insights for many molecules. Our motivation is to demonstrate the step-change in drug disovery that is already evident in this data, and invite attention to the breakthroughs possible by scaling to millions and 10's of millions of molecules

The five preprints

Five preprints, one discovery question each.

The papers track the questions a discovery team has to answer in order. Can we measure what a molecule did. Can we learn from that measurement. Does the learning survive new chemistry. Does it transfer across cell types. Can it point toward a mechanism.

PAPER 01 - ActiveSeq
Image and RNA from the same microwell.
Each well is imaged and sequenced, so a hit comes with both a picture of the cells and an RNA fingerprint that helps explain the response. On the 35-compound control panel, image plus RNA classified compounds better than either readout on its own.
Read the non-technical summary
PAPER 02 - Generative Chemistry
The screen learns which chemistry moves which biology.
Every Z-Screen compound is made from known building blocks. With that recipe attached to each RNA readout, the model can learn which pieces drive which cell programs. In the best-sampled libraries it predicted the RNA response of compounds it had never been shown.
Read the non-technical summary
PAPER 03 - Generalization Ladder
Is the model learning, or copying close cousins?
Five tests of increasing difficulty separate real prediction from near-neighbor lookup. In ZEL031/THP1 the model still helped when whole chemical pieces were held out. In ZEL024/HEK293 it mostly filled gaps inside an already dense grid. The hardest scaffold-hop test is open and now precisely defined.
Read the non-technical summary
PAPER 04 - Cross-Cell Transfer
Measure in one cell type, predict in a calibrated pair.
Running every compound in every cell type is infeasible. With paired data across two cell lines, Z-Screen learns how a chemical program in one cell line looks in another and uses that to predict drug activity at scale.
Read the non-technical summary
PAPER 05 - Causal Reasoning
Each hit lands with a mechanism hypothesis attached.
Every compound has an RNA fingerprint, and so does every gene perturbation in a public CRISPR atlas. Searching one against the other turns an anonymous phenotypic hit into a ranked list of candidate mechanisms. A striking example: two unrelated chemistries in different cell lines both pointed to identical biology. Is it patent-busting if the compounds don't look alike?

Pilot scope

This is sample data.

The pilot used a thin slice of what the chip can run. Even so, this limited campaign already produced reproducible cell responses, usable predictions on held-out chemistry, cross-cell transfer when there is sufficient training data, and CRISPR-linked mechanism hypotheses. With the infrastructure now in place, we ask - what if this can be scaled 1000-fold?
