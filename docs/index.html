Z-Screen - Pilot Release - April 2026

A small Z-Screen pilot, and what the data already say.

Z-Screen makes new molecules and puts each one in front of living cells on the same chip. The five preprints walk through what came out of one pilot dataset: how we read each response, how the chemistry began to predict biology, how far that prediction held when the chemistry changed, whether responses transferred across cell types, and what the hits looked like next to public CRISPR data.

About this release

Drug discovery usually forces a choice between breadth and depth: screen many compounds shallowly, or study a few in detail. Z-Screen does both in the same experiment. Every well on the chip contains a small group of cells and one bead carrying one compound. The chip images those cells, reads their RNA, and keeps the compound's identity tied to both readouts the entire time.

The pilot is small. The signal across these five papers is broader: reproducible cell responses, predictions that beat simple baselines on held-out chemistry, partial cross-cell transfer, and CRISPR-linked mechanism hypotheses for unannotated hits. The data and reproduction scripts are public on Zenodo, and we are circulating the preprints before formal bioRxiv posting so the work can be pressure-tested. More on the platform →

The five preprints

Five preprints, one discovery question each.

The papers track the questions a discovery team has to answer in order. Can we measure what a molecule did. Can we learn from that measurement. Does the learning survive new chemistry. Does it transfer across cell types. Can it point toward a mechanism.

PAPER 01 - ActiveSeq
Image and RNA from the same microwell.
Each well is imaged and sequenced, so a hit comes with both a picture of the cells and an RNA fingerprint that helps explain the response. On the 35-compound control panel, image plus RNA classified compounds better than either readout on its own.
Read the non-technical summary
PAPER 02 - Generative Chemistry
The screen learns which chemistry moves which biology.
Every Z-Screen compound is made from known building blocks. With that recipe attached to each RNA readout, the model can learn which pieces drive which cell programs. In the best-sampled libraries it predicted the RNA response of compounds it had never been shown.
Read the non-technical summary
PAPER 03 - Generalization Ladder
Is the model learning, or copying close cousins?
Five tests of increasing difficulty separate real prediction from near-neighbor lookup. In ZEL031/THP1 the model still helped when whole chemical pieces were held out. In ZEL024/HEK293 it mostly filled gaps inside an already dense grid. The hardest scaffold-hop test is open and now precisely defined.
Read the non-technical summary
PAPER 04 - Cross-Cell Transfer
Measure once, then choose the next cell to test in.
Running every compound in every cell type gets expensive. With paired data across two cell lines, Z-Screen learns how a chemical program in one cell line looks in another and uses that to prioritize the next round of wet-lab work.
Read the non-technical summary
PAPER 05 - Causal Reasoning
Each hit lands with a mechanism hypothesis attached.
Every compound has an RNA fingerprint, and so does every gene perturbation in a public CRISPR atlas. Searching one against the other turns an anonymous phenotypic hit into a ranked list of candidate mechanisms. The most striking example: two unrelated chemistries in different cell lines both pointed to V-ATPase biology.
Read the non-technical summary
Independent validation

Checks that the map is seeing real biology.

A fair reader will ask whether all of this is a clever analysis of one dataset. The release includes four checks aimed at that question: agreement with an outside RNA reference, a known positive control, small-molecule signatures that follow paired-CRISPR logic, and two unrelated routes into the same V-ATPase biology.

EXTERNAL REFERENCE
Z-Screen agrees with an outside RNA reference.
For the compounds that overlap with LINCS L1000, Z-Screen RNA signatures point in the same direction. The overlap is small, but every well-covered matched comparison came back positive without any Z-Screen-specific tuning.
See the cross-platform test
INTERNAL LOGIC
Chemistry follows paired-CRISPR logic.
In the Norman atlas, a CRISPR double perturbation should sit between the two singles that make it up. Z-Screen chemical signatures showed that same relationship, and the pattern repeated in a second library across three cell lines.
See the combinatorial test
POSITIVE CONTROL
A known BRD4 drug points back to BRD4.
MZ1 is a known BRD4 degrader. When its Z-Screen signature was searched against a matched THP1 CRISPR panel, BRD4 came back as the top calibrated match.
See the positive control
DISCOVERY EXAMPLE
Two separate paths point to V-ATPase biology.
BAY-293 produced a V-ATPase and vesicle-trafficking signature in A549. Separately, an unrelated ZEL028-2 chemistry in HEK293 surfaced ATP6V1A with independent subset support. Different chemistry, different cell context, same biological neighborhood.
See the cross-system rediscovery
Pilot scope

This is what we got at small scale.

The pilot used a thin slice of what the chip can run. Even so, a single limited campaign already produced reproducible cell responses, usable predictions on held-out chemistry, partial cross-cell transfer, and CRISPR-linked mechanism hypotheses. The next question is what the same loop produces at full campaign scale.
