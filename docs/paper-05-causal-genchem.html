PAPER 05 - Causal Genetics

Every compound nominates its likely target, automatically.

Target deconvolution is the most expensive bottleneck in phenotypic discovery. We turn it into a search problem. 8,599 chemistry tuples are scanned against 8,603 genome-wide CRISPR knockouts, with permutation-null calibration on every match. 1,276 chemistries return a calibrated CRISPR-like mechanism hypothesis at q ≤ 0.05 across 1,714 distinct programs. The framework recovers BRD4 from a known BRD4 PROTAC, surfaces a coherent V-ATPase program for an SOS1 inhibitor, and tracks combinatorial logic across all 131 paired CRISPRa Norman doubles.

Hero figure - Calibrated chemistry-to-CRISPR hits from the headline ZEL024 / HEK293 scan, with two matched-cell THP1 anchors. Discovery hit (BAY-293 to V-ATPase) and positive control (MZ1 to BRD4) both clear strict permutation-null calibration.
TL;DR

Phenotypic screens find drugs without knowing the target, then spend months figuring out what the drug actually does. This paper turns that into a search problem at chemistry resolution. The headline scan (8,599 chemistry tuples by 8,603 Replogle K562 knockouts, ~74M chemistry-by-CRISPR pair scores, permutation-null calibrated) returns 1,276 calibrated tuples covering 1,714 distinct CRISPR programs. The framework recovers BRD4 from MZ1 (a known BRD4 PROTAC), surfaces a V-ATPase program for the SOS1 inhibitor BAY-293 (q = 0.003, top hit z = 11.86), and tracks combinatorial logic across all 131 Norman CRISPRa doubles.

Why it matters

Target deconvolution (answering "what is this compound doing?") is the most expensive bottleneck in phenotypic drug discovery. Affinity pull-downs, photoaffinity labeling, and thermal shift assays are slow, biased toward abundant binders, and consume months and millions per program. The Connectivity Map and LINCS L1000 program established the chemistry-side framework for searching this space, but L1000 records one profile per compound. It cannot decompose a hit into the structural pieces that drove the response.

Genetic perturbation atlases are reference dictionaries. Each entry describes the transcriptional state that results from removing or activating a gene. If a chemical perturbation produces a state that matches a particular genetic entry, that entry becomes a mechanism hypothesis, automatically, for every screened compound. This paper shows the dictionary works at chemistry resolution. Z-Screen reads the transcriptional fingerprint of an individual combinatorial tuple and points to the genetic perturbation it most resembles, with calibrated false-discovery control. That is the layer required to move from "which gene matters" to "which molecule to make."

What we did

We compared Z-Screen rank signatures, built at the level of individual combinatorial tuples where coverage allows, against three CRISPR reference atlases: 8,603 genome-wide CRISPR-KO signatures from Replogle K562, 236 CRISPRa signatures (105 single + 131 paired) from Norman K562, and 78 THP1 CRISPR perturbations from scPerturb (Papalexi / Satija ECCITE-seq plus Wessels combinatorial CRISPR-Cas13).

The headline scan compared 8,599 distinct ZEL024 / HEK293 chemistry tuples (4-position bb0/bb1/bb2/bb3 combinations) against the 8,603 Replogle K562 perturbations: roughly 74 million chemistry-by-CRISPR pair scores. Each comparison used direction-aware rank overlap on the top 250 up- and top 250 down-regulated genes. The top 10,000 rows by net mimic overlap were calibrated against permutation-null gene sets matched in size to each CRISPR signature, with 1,000 random draws per row, converted to Benjamini-Hochberg q-values.

We then asked five questions: does chemistry resolution matter (does single-BB averaging recover the same CRISPR matches as full tuple resolution)? Does the framework recover documented targets in matched-cell positive controls? Does the leaderboard concentrate on coherent biology rather than recurrent hubs? Do unannotated chemistries surface plausible mechanism hypotheses beyond primary pharmacology? And does chemistry-resolved tuple signal track the same combinatorial structure the Norman CRISPR-activation atlas was built to express?

What we found

Seven lines of evidence. One conclusion: combinatorial chemistry is searchable over the genome.

FINDING 01

The headline scan returns 1,276 chemistries, 1,714 CRISPR programs, q ≤ 0.05.

From the 8,599 ZEL024 / HEK293 tuples by 8,603 Replogle K562 KOs scan (~74 million chemistry-by-CRISPR pair scores), the calibrated top 10,000 rows are dominated by hits at q ≤ 0.05 across 1,276 distinct chemical tuples and 1,714 distinct CRISPR programs. The biology concentrates: DNA replication (MCM2, SLBP, CHAF1B), mitochondrial function (MRPL3, COX17, ATP5F1A), trafficking and nonsense-mediated decay (EFR3A, DDOST, SMG5).

FINDING 02

Matched-cell positive control: MZ1 recovers BRD4.

MZ1 is a small-molecule PROTAC that selectively degrades BRD4. Scanned against the matched-cell THP1 CRISPR panel, Z-Screen recovers BRD4 as the top calibrated CRISPR-like signature (net z = 6.84, empirical q = 0.015). Subsequent neighbors fall in the BRD4-adjacent transcription-elongation and chromatin co-regulator programs (mediator, FACT, EP300, SMARCD1). Coherent biology, not coincidental overlap.

FINDING 03

Discovery: BAY-293 surfaces a V-ATPase / trafficking program.

BAY-293 is an SOS1 inhibitor profiled in A549. The top 12 calibrated Replogle matches all sit at empirical q = 0.003, with net z between 9.5 and 11.9. They concentrate on the V-ATPase complex (ATP6V1A, ATP6V1H, ATP6V1B2, ATP6V1C1, ATP6V1E1) and adjacent vesicular-trafficking machinery (ZW10, SACM1L, TMED2, ZNHIT1, CLTC). Top hit ZW10 reaches z = 11.86. Not a hypothesis a target-based search would have produced.

z = 11.86BAY-293 to ZW10 - q = 0.003
FINDING 04

Chemistry follows CRISPR combinatorial logic.

For all 131 paired CRISPR-activation perturbations in the Norman atlas, Z-Screen tuple-vs-double match scores correlate positively with the sum of tuple-vs-single match scores. Median Spearman ρ = 0.353, every double significant at p ≤ 0.05, strongest pairs reach ρ between 0.60 and 0.69. For 98.5% of doubles, the best Z-Screen tuple's score against the double exceeded its score against either constituent single. The chemistry tracks the same combinatorial structure the Norman atlas was built to express. L1000, a one-profile-per-compound reference, cannot produce that result.

131 / 131Norman doubles - all positive Spearman
FINDING 05

Hubs flagged, not laundered: 1,236 / 1,664 still pass after stripping.

Broadly responsive CRISPR knockouts can match many transcriptional states by being broadly responsive themselves. A benchmark that does not control for that property will look stronger than it is. We reran the calibrated table after dropping the 50 most-recurrent CRISPR programs. The diversity holds: 1,236 distinct chemistry tuples and 1,664 distinct CRISPR programs still pass q ≤ 0.05. The headline is not driven by a small handful of recurrent hubs.

1,236 / 1,664After hub-strip - q ≤ 0.05
FINDING 06

Documented targets recover at genome-wide scale, with informative failures.

Among 29 Z-Screen compounds with a documented primary target represented in the Replogle K562 panel, 12 had their target ranked in the top 15% of the cross-cell genome-wide ranking. Derazantinib hit FGFR1 at rank 145 of 8,839 (~1.6%); STC-15 hit METTL3 at ~9% in A549. The failures are mechanistically informative: cobimetinib's MAP2K2 ranked at 4,694 (allosteric MEK inhibition does not always produce a knockout-like transcriptional consequence), and GSK126's EZH2 was anti-correlated rather than mimicking (consistent with the long half-life of EZH2 protein under enzymatic inhibition).

12 / 29Documented targets in top 15%
FINDING 07

The framework replicates on a second combinatorial library, across three cell lines.

The same scan, rerun on the ZEL028-2 library (a 4-position library with no overlap in building-block vocabulary with ZEL024) at full-tuple resolution, returns a calibrated top-10,000 table covering 943 distinct chemistry tuples and 285 distinct CRISPR programs at q ≤ 0.05 in HEK293. The strongest match reaches net z = 24.7 (vs 20.5 in A549 and 18.6 in H1650). The biology themes that surfaced in ZEL024 / HEK293 reappear: RNA processing (DICER1, DROSHA, the EXOSC exosome family, CNOT3, SMG5) and mitochondrial translation (MRPL family, GFM1, IARS2). 15 CRISPR programs appear in the top-50 deduplicated hits across all three ZEL028-2 cell lines. Per-tuple coverage in ZEL028-2 is shallower than in ZEL024 (median 2 vs 13 cells per tuple), so individual tuple signatures are noisier and these results read as a chemistry-resolved leaderboard rather than per-tuple definitive calls. The result is not about any individual tuple call. It is about running the same scan on a different chemical grammar in three different cell lines, with the same calibration, and seeing the same biology come back.

What this enables

A chemical search engine over genetic perturbation space.

Query a CRISPR target. Return a ranked, calibrated list of chemistry that pushes cells toward the same transcriptional state, with full per-position building-block provenance for every hit. That is the deliverable, and it is a different object from a similarity score against a single compound database. For a discovery team starting from a functional-genomics screen, the framework converts genetic-state nominations into chemistry to make. For a phenotypic team holding an unannotated hit, it generates a calibrated mechanism hypothesis at compound resolution. Combined with paper 04's cross-cell transfer, the mapping reaches into cell types where direct K562 or THP1 reference comparisons are not yet possible. The headline scan reproduces on a second combinatorial library across three cell lines, with the same calibration, surfacing the same biology themes (RNA processing, mitochondrial translation): the chemistry-to-CRISPR map is real, and it is repeatable.
