PAPER 05 - Causal Reasoning

From anonymous hit to testable mechanism hypothesis.

Phenotypic screens can find interesting compounds before anyone knows what they do. The painful part comes next: figuring out the biology. This paper compares each compound's RNA fingerprint with public CRISPR fingerprints, turning a hit into a ranked list of genetic programs it resembles. The result is not automatic target proof; it is a much faster starting point for mechanism work.

TL;DR

Every compound produces an RNA fingerprint. Public CRISPR atlases provide fingerprints for what happens when genes are perturbed. Paper 05 puts those two dictionaries in the same search frame. In the headline scan, 1,276 chemical tuples received calibrated CRISPR-like hypotheses. Known controls behaved sensibly, and two unrelated paths converged on V-ATPase biology.

Why it matters

Phenotypic discovery is powerful because it can find compounds from function, even when the target is unknown. But that strength creates the next bottleneck: after a hit appears, teams can spend months asking what the compound is doing.

CRISPR atlases are like dictionaries of cell states. Each entry says what the cell looks like when a gene is removed or activated. If a compound creates a similar RNA state, that genetic entry becomes a mechanism hypothesis. Z-Screen makes this especially useful because the hypothesis is attached to a specific combinatorial molecule and its building-block recipe.

What we did

We converted Z-Screen compound responses and public CRISPR perturbations into comparable ranked gene signatures. Then we asked which CRISPR state each compound most resembled.

The headline scan compared 8,599 ZEL024 / HEK293 chemistry tuples with 8,603 Replogle K562 CRISPR knockout signatures, then calibrated the top matches against matched random gene sets. Additional checks used Norman CRISPR-activation doubles and matched THP1 CRISPR controls.

The questions were practical: does full-molecule resolution matter, do known controls recover known biology, does the leaderboard avoid being dominated by generic hubs, do unannotated compounds produce plausible hypotheses, and does the chemistry follow the logic of genetic combinations?

What we found

Nine lines of evidence for a chemical-genetic search map.

FINDING 01

The headline scan produces a broad calibrated hypothesis table.

The ZEL024 / HEK293 scan linked 1,276 distinct chemical tuples to 1,714 distinct CRISPR programs at the calibrated threshold. The matches concentrated in interpretable biology, including DNA replication, mitochondrial function, trafficking, and RNA surveillance.

1,276 / 1,714Chemistries / CRISPR programs
FINDING 02

A known BRD4 degrader points back to BRD4.

MZ1 is designed to degrade BRD4. Against a matched THP1 CRISPR panel, BRD4 returned as the top calibrated match, with neighboring programs that fit BRD4 biology.

BRD4Top matched-cell hit for MZ1
FINDING 03

BAY-293 reveals biology beyond its primary annotation.

BAY-293 is known as an SOS1 inhibitor, but its RNA state pointed to a coherent V-ATPase and vesicle-trafficking neighborhood. That is the kind of secondary biology a phenotypic platform should be able to surface.

V-ATPaseBAY-293 discovery signal
FINDING 04

Chemical signatures follow genetic-combination logic.

The Norman CRISPR atlas contains paired gene perturbations. Z-Screen tuple scores tracked the expected relationship between doubles and their two singles, and the pattern reproduced in a second library across three cell lines. That argues the map is reading structured biology, not just noise.

12 / 12ZEL028-2 panels reproduce - 90.8 to 100% of Norman doubles significant
FINDING 05

Generic CRISPR hubs are flagged, not hidden.

Some CRISPR perturbations match many states because they are broadly responsive. After removing the 50 most recurrent programs, the table still contained broad chemistry and CRISPR diversity, so the headline was not driven by a few generic hubs.

1,236 / 1,664After removing common hubs
FINDING 06

Known targets recover when biology makes them visible.

Documented targets were enriched in genome-wide rankings, but not universally recovered. The misses are informative: a drug can act through a target without producing a knockout-like RNA state in that cell context.

12 / 29Documented targets in top 15%
FINDING 07

A second library produces a stricter shortlist.

ZEL028-2 had shallower per-tuple coverage, so the analysis used independent chemistry subsets as supporting evidence. Two non-hub candidates passed the strictest corroborated tier: HEK293 ATP6V1A and A549 FKBP9, with a broader follow-up queue behind them.

2 + 11Strict shortlist + broader queue
FINDING 08

Two unrelated paths converge on V-ATPase biology.

This is the strongest internal validation signal. BAY-293 in A549 pointed to V-ATPase and trafficking biology. Separately, an unrelated ZEL028-2 tuple in HEK293 surfaced ATP6V1A with subset support. Different chemistry, different cell context, same biological neighborhood.

2 PATHSBAY-293 / A549 + ZEL028-2 / HEK293 - one V-ATPase mechanism
FINDING 09

The scan works again in a second chemical grammar.

ZEL028-2 reproduced several biology themes from the headline library across three cell lines, including RNA-processing and mitochondrial-translation programs. Because tuple coverage was shallower, these results are treated as a ranked chemistry-resolved leaderboard rather than finished mechanism calls.

943 / 285Second-library chemistry / programs
What this enables

A search engine from genetic state to chemistry.

For a team starting from functional genomics, the long-term promise is direct: query a desired genetic state and return chemistry that pushes cells toward a similar RNA program. For a team starting from a phenotypic hit, the promise runs the other way: ask which genetic perturbations the hit most resembles. The pilot does not make target validation disappear, but it can move mechanism work from a blank page to a ranked, calibrated, chemistry-resolved hypothesis list.
