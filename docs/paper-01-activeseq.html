PAPER 01 - ActiveSeq

We see what a compound does and understand why, at the same time.

No public phenotypic platform pairs imaging with transcriptomics at this density. ActiveSeq does both, in the same microwell, at 50,000 wells per chip. Across 20,222 matched wells that we are free to share, same-well fusion is strongly additive: combined features improve recall on every one of 35 control compounds, and the pilot lands in the same range as the strongest public multimodal benchmark on an order of magnitude fewer cells.

TL;DR

Same-well image-plus-RNA pairing produces an additive prediction gain across all 35 control compounds. Pooled balanced accuracy moves from 41.4% (RNA alone) to 55.5% with merged features. Once normalized for task difficulty (35 compounds here vs 28 in scGeneScope), the pilot reaches 19.4× chance against scGeneScope's 18.0× multimodal reference, on an order of magnitude fewer cells.

Why it matters

Drug discovery has always traded throughput for mechanistic resolution. Imaging scales but only sees half the story. Single-cell sequencing reveals mechanism but cost per compound keeps it out of reach at library scale. Treatment-matched public datasets like scGeneScope pair the two modalities across separate populations of cells exposed to the same drug; the alignment is at the treatment level, not the cellular level.

ActiveSeq does both at the same density, inside the same well. A Z-Screen hit comes with an image (what happened) and an RNA profile (why) from the same multicellular colony. No follow-up assay. No cross-replicate alignment.

What we did

Two ActiveSeq pilot runs (ACT010 and ACT011), 20,222 matched wells. 11,511 wells carried a 35-compound control panel. The other 8,711 were dummy-bead wells (no compound), retained as a built-in negative-control stress test on assay chemistry.

For every well we extracted a per-well image vector and a per-well RNA profile from the same physical microwell. Four questions structured the analysis. Does the assay flag chemistry failures in the readout itself? Are matched compound measurements reproducible within run and transferable across runs? Do image and RNA preserve a shared map of compound relationships? And does same-well pairing actually improve compound classification on held-out wells?

The reference benchmark is scGeneScope, a treatment-matched (not same-well) Cell Painting plus scRNA-seq dataset across 28 treatments in U2-OS cells with ~716,767 single-cell images and ~627,704 single-cell RNA profiles. We size every result against that reference and against a 2.9% top-1 chance level on the 35-compound panel.

What we found

Five results that turn same-well multimodality from possible into operational.

FINDING 01

Failed chemistry shows up in the readout itself.

Median UMIs collapse from ~5,000 to ~300 on dummy-bead wells. Productive control-panel wells keep full RNA depth. Operators flag chemistry failures directly; they never silently contaminate downstream biology.

FINDING 02

Compound identity is reproducible within run and transferable across runs.

Within-run split-half top-1 retrieval: 98.8% RNA, 94.2% image (ACT010). Compound centroids learned in ACT010 retrieve the same compounds in ACT011 at 85.7% top-1 in calibrated image space, against a 2.9% chance level.

85.7% / 100%Cross-run top-1 / top-3 - image
FINDING 03

Image and RNA preserve a shared compound map. Gene programs stay portable.

Pairwise compound-distance correlation between modalities: ρ = 0.718 in ACT010, 0.607 in ACT011. The two readouts rank compounds the same way. Centered pseudobulk gene signatures retrieve the correct compound across runs at 48.6% top-1 and 68.6% top-3, with coherent compound-specific gene programs (palbociclib, BAY-293, rucaparib, crizotinib, GSK126).

ρ = 0.718Image ↔ RNA agreement
FINDING 04

Same-well multimodality is strongly additive.

Pooled balanced accuracy on held-out wells: 29.2% image alone, 41.4% RNA alone, 55.5% merged. Imaging contributes orthogonal signal even when paired with the stronger RNA branch. Every one of 35 compounds gains.

FINDING 05

The right comparison normalizes for task difficulty. We win it.

Different studies use different label spaces (35 compounds here, 28 in the closest public benchmark scGeneScope), so raw accuracy isn't directly comparable. Fold-over-chance is. Pooled multimodal performance reaches 19.4x chance, against 18.0x for the scGeneScope multimodal multiprofile reference. The strongest run reaches 21.3x chance, on an order of magnitude fewer single-cell observations.

What this enables

Same-well pairing reveals causal relationships.

For a discovery team, ActiveSeq turns the Z-Screen microchip into a same-well multimodal phenotype engine. Imaging provides functional readout, transcriptomics provides mechanism, and the two are coupled at the level of the same multicellular colony rather than at treatment-matched populations. That coupling is the property most likely to deliver breakthroughs for complex diseases, where mapping molecular insights to rare functional observations unlocks druggability. The natural extensions to this pilot follow directly: enlarge the control panel, broaden cell-line coverage, continue collecting matched same-well pairs at chip-design throughput. Each investment compounds a signal that is already visible.
