PAPER 02 - Generative Chemistry

A screen that learns from every molecule it tests.

Most screens end as a spreadsheet of hits and misses. Z-Screen can leave behind something more useful: a map connecting chemical building blocks to the cell states they produce. In the best-sampled pilot systems, that map predicted responses for held-out compounds and agreed with an outside public RNA reference where the same compounds overlapped.

TL;DR

Because Z-Screen knows the recipe for every compound, the dataset can ask which chemical pieces move which cell programs. In the strongest pilot case, the model predicted held-out RNA states substantially better than a simple baseline. That turns a screen from a static list into a reusable design map.

Why it matters

A normal screen can tell you which wells looked interesting. It often cannot tell you why one chemical series worked, which pieces of the molecule mattered, or what to make next.

Z-Screen keeps the chemistry recipe attached to the RNA response. That means the data can become memory: the system can learn that certain building blocks tend to push cells toward certain states, then use that lesson to prioritize compounds that have not yet been tested. That is the platform value in this paper.

What we did

We analyzed 615,793 RNA profiles across 12 combinatorial libraries and 4 cell lines. Each profile was linked to the compound's building-block identity, so the model could learn from the structure of the chemistry rather than treating every molecule as an isolated label.

The analysis asked whether the RNA signal was reproducible, whether building blocks predicted held-out compound responses, whether full chemical structure added extra information, whether new tuples landed near known reference drugs, whether programs recurred across cell lines, and whether imaging added a secondary signal.

We also compared overlapping control compounds with LINCS L1000, the major public transcriptional reference. That check is important because it asks whether Z-Screen is seeing biology the broader field can recognize.

What we found

Six results that turn a screen into a learning model.

FINDING 01

The readout is stable enough to model.

Named controls reproduced strongly across all four cell lines. That establishes the floor: when a model misses, the first explanation is not simply that the RNA readout is too noisy.

0.99+Control reproducibility - 3/4 cell lines
FINDING 02

The chemical recipe predicts biology.

In the strongest systems, building-block models predicted held-out compound RNA states better than simple baselines. The practical message is that the model can learn which chemical pieces carry recurring biological programs.

-33.2%Prediction error reduction
FINDING 03

Full structure can add extra signal.

Building-block identity was the most reliable coordinate. In the best-sampled setting, adding full chemical-structure information improved the model further, suggesting a path from library grammar toward broader chemical reasoning as coverage grows.

-29.3%Error with recipe + structure
FINDING 04

New library compounds land near recognizable drug states.

Some library tuples produced RNA neighborhoods near known reference compounds. These are not mechanism claims by themselves, but they are exactly the kind of candidate neighborhoods a discovery team can triage and test.

0.901Cosine to ZF-104 - ZEL024 / HEK293
FINDING 05

Some chemical programs survive a change in cell type.

At chemistry-resolved positions, several building-block programs pointed in similar directions across paired cell lines. The transfer is not perfect, but it is enough to justify asking how far chemical programs can be reused.

0.602Cross-cell agreement signal
FINDING 06

An outside reference sees the same direction.

For overlapping compounds, Z-Screen RNA signatures agreed with LINCS L1000 without Z-Screen-specific calibration. The overlap is limited, but it is an important sanity check that the pilot is capturing recognizable transcriptional biology.

What this enables

A design map is the platform's compounding layer.

Once a screen learns a relationship between chemistry and cell state, each campaign can inform the next one. That map is what Paper 03 stress-tests on harder chemistry, what Paper 04 uses to project responses across cell types, and what Paper 05 connects to CRISPR atlases. The long-term value is not one ranked list; it is a reusable memory of how chemistry moves biology.
