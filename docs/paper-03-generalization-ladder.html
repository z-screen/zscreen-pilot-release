PAPER 03 - Generalization Ladder

Predictions hold up on genuinely new chemistry, not just close copies.

ML claims in drug discovery often hide retrieval. A five-rung ladder separates interpolation from real extrapolation, with a chemistry-embedding nearest-neighbor baseline at every rung. Under strict multi-axis holdout, where every substantive building block in the test compound is unseen during training, Z-Screen still beats retrieval on 90% of compounds at a +0.168 cosine margin. The model is learning chemistry, not memorizing it.

Hero figure - Median cosine across the ladder. L3 (strict multi-axis holdout) is the cleanest evidence of genuine extrapolation in the dataset.
TL;DR

Under strict multi-axis chemistry holdout in ZEL031 / THP1, the model retains 0.765 cosine to held-out transcriptomic states, beats chemistry-embedding nearest-neighbor retrieval by 0.168, and wins on 90% of test compounds. Single-position holdouts confirm the same regime at 89-91% per-compound win rate. The hardest rung (cross-library scaffold-family transfer) becomes the next experiment to run, not a ceiling on the platform.

Why it matters

Most ML claims in drug discovery conflate different notions of generalization. Predicting an unseen combination of familiar chemical pieces is a different problem from predicting a compound built from unseen pieces. Both differ from predicting a new scaffold family entirely. The recent BELKA literature on DNA-encoded libraries was the first careful demonstration that ligand-only models trained on DEL binary binder labels weaken sharply once chemistry moves away from training, and most splits in the ML literature since have been unable to distinguish real generalization from nearest-neighbor retrieval.

This paper imposes the distinction explicitly on dense phenotypic data. Five rungs of difficulty. A chemistry-embedding nearest-neighbor retrieval baseline at every rung. And a separate verdict on each rung: does the platform extrapolate, does it interpolate well, or does it need more data. The rungs that matter come back positive. The honest negative on the hardest rung becomes a defined next experiment, not a hidden caveat.

What we did

We built a five-level evaluation ladder. L1 holds out unseen tuples within a shared building-block vocabulary. L2 holds out a subset of building-block identities at one position. L3 holds out identities at every substantive position simultaneously, so every building block in the test compound is unseen during training. L4 holds out chemical-neighborhood clusters within a library, seeded by greedy farthest-point sampling so the test sets are diverse rather than locally similar. L5 trains on one library family and tests on another in the same cell line.

Every rung uses the same toolkit. A 256-dim per-compound chemistry embedding (a fixed-seed Gaussian random projection of 2048-bit ECFP4 fingerprints, irreversible without the projection matrix). Ridge regression at α = 10. A train-mean baseline. And the one that matters: a chemistry-embedding nearest-neighbor retrieval baseline at every rung. Without it, a nominally hard split can be silently solved by copying the closest measured analog.

What we found

The ladder separates the easy claim from the hard one.

FINDING 01

Strict multi-axis holdout: real extrapolation in ZEL031 / THP1.

Under L3, every substantive building-block axis is unseen during training. The model retains 0.765 cosine against 0.597 for retrieval (a +0.168 delta). It wins on 90% of test compounds, and all 10 random draws are positive under paired bootstrap. Median nearest-training cosine in the public chemistry-embedding space sits at 0.642, so the chemistry is well separated from the training set. Retrieval cannot account for the gain.

+0.168Cosine over retrieval - L3
FINDING 02

Single-position holdouts confirm the same regime.

L2 in the same system: bb0 holdout reaches 0.783 cosine vs 0.632 retrieval (+0.151) on 728 test compounds at win rate 89%. bb1 holdout reaches 0.790 vs 0.644 (+0.146) on 729 test compounds at win rate 91%. All 10 random draws positive under paired bootstrap intervals at both positions.

89 - 91%Per-compound win rate - L2
FINDING 03

ZEL024 / HEK293 is matrix completion in a saturated grid.

Median nearest-training cosine on this library's holdouts sits at 0.881 to 0.988 in the chemistry-embedding space. Many "held-out" compounds still have near-duplicate cousins in the training set. The ladder catches that: ZEL024 / HEK293 delivers high-quality matrix completion inside a 13,769-of-13,944 saturated combinatorial grid. Useful for prioritizing chemistry within a design space, but not extrapolation, and we report it that way.

13,769 / 13,944Observed tuples - saturated grid
FINDING 04

Chemical-neighborhood holdout still beats retrieval.

L4 holds chemistry at arm's length while staying inside one reaction grammar. The clustering is seeded by greedy farthest-point sampling, so the test sets are diverse rather than locally similar. The model carries +0.236 cosine over retrieval in ZEL024 and +0.155 in ZEL031, on average cluster sizes of 2,754 and 711 compounds.

+0.236L4 model gain - ZEL024
FINDING 05

Cross-library scaffold transfer becomes the next experiment.

L5 is weak on retrospective data (best absolute cosine 0.194, best gain over retrieval +0.096 in the H1650 ZEL024 to ZEL028-2 transfer). The ladder is meant to surface this honestly, not to reframe the benchmark to hide it. A single prospective same-cell scaffold-hop chip run (~1,000 wells) with parity coverage and a pre-registered model would settle the cross-library question.

~1,000 wellsOne chip - settles L5
What this enables

A model that survives held-out chemistry is the only kind worth trusting.

A discovery team only gets value out of a chemistry-to-phenotype model when the model still helps after the chemistry changes. The ladder defined here is the cleanest current benchmark we know of for that question on dense phenotypic data, and it produces two complementary verdicts depending on the library: ZEL024 / HEK293 delivers high-quality matrix completion within a saturated combinatorial grid (useful for prioritizing chemistry inside an explored design space), while ZEL031 / THP1 delivers genuine out-of-vocabulary extrapolation. The honest call on the hardest rung also defines a single chip run that would convert L5 from retrospective ambiguity to a definitive prospective answer. The ladder ships alongside the preprint as a reusable benchmark.
